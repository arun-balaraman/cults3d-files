Geneva Dice Stepper - print settings and assembly
=================================================
Turn the handle round and round: the pin on the orange driver drops into a slot,
flicks the 6-slot Geneva wheel exactly one sixth of a turn (the next dice face lines
up with the arrow) and then the locking disc holds it solid until the next turn.
The same intermittent motion used in film projectors and old watches.

Parts (print in the STL orientation, no supports, no glue):
  base.stl          131 x 99 x 15 mm  ~36 g  (two axle posts)
  driver.stl         51 x 40 x 29 mm  ~9 g   (pin, locking disc and handle)
  geneva_wheel.stl   81 x 89 x 10 mm  ~14 g  (prints face down: the dice pips are on the bed side)
Total about 60 g of PLA and about 2 h on a Creality K1C.

Settings: PLA, 0.2 mm layers, 3 walls, 20% infill, no supports.
Two colours look great (swap filament between parts). First layer squish matters for the
wheel's pips; if the pips close up, reduce elephant-foot compensation.

Assembly:
  1. Drop the wheel onto the post at the round end of the base, pips up.
  2. Drop the driver onto the other post, pin side towards the wheel.
     Turn the driver until its round locking disc sits in one of the wheel's curved notches.
  3. Turn the handle anticlockwise (seen from above). One full turn = one step of the wheel.
Nothing snaps in: gravity holds the parts, so you can lift them off any time.

Clearances: 0.5 mm round the pin in the slots, 0.6-0.7 mm between the locking disc and
the wheel, 0.45 mm on the axles, 0.6 mm above the base.

Fresh design that hasn't been test-printed yet - please share a make!
