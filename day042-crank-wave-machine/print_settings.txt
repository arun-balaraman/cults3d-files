Crank Wave Machine - print settings and assembly
================================================
Turn the hand wheel: a twisted eccentric camshaft lifts 12 sliders one after another and the tiles on top
roll like an ocean wave (one full wave across the machine, 15 mm rise and fall).

Parts (print 1 of each, except the slider):
  frame.stl               base + two towers, prints as is                      ~74 g
  guide_beam.stl          prints upside down as exported (tongues up)           ~24 g
  twisted_camshaft.stl    prints standing up, 195 mm tall: USE A BRIM (5-8 mm)  ~29 g
  journal_pin.stl         prints as exported (round end down)                    ~1 g
  slider_print_12.stl     PRINT 12, tile flat on the bed                         ~4 g each
  hand_wheel.stl          prints flat, knob up                                   ~7 g
Total about 180 g and 7 h. Everything fits a 220 x 220 bed.

Settings: PLA, 0.2 mm layers, 3 walls, 15% infill, NO supports. Camshaft: 3 walls, 20% infill, brim,
slow outer walls a little for the tall part. Two colours look great (blue sliders, orange camshaft) but
one colour works fine.

Assembly (no glue, no screws)
1. Push the journal pin's D end into the D socket in the flat end of the camshaft (snug fit; a drop of
   superglue is optional if yours is loose).
2. Lay the camshaft in the frame: the pin drops into the U slot of the left tower, the round journal at the
   other end into the right tower's slot. The D shaft end sticks out on the right.
3. Set the guide beam on the tower tops so its two tongues slide down into the slots: they hold the shaft
   in place.
4. Drop the 12 sliders, rounded foot first, through the square holes in the beam. They rest on the camshaft.
5. Push the hand wheel onto the D end. Turn and watch the wave.
Tip: a little dry PTFE lube or candle wax on the camshaft makes it glide.
